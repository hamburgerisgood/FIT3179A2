ARIA Music Awards · 1987 to 2025

Big Smoke, Back Paddock

Australia's biggest music awards like to say they celebrate the whole country. We mapped the hometown of every winner of three headline awards to see how far the trophies really travel.

By Lee Wei Han · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2, Monash University · Published 12 October 2026

Awards shape which artists get radio play, festival slots and funding. If the winners mostly come from a few cities, young musicians everywhere else see fewer people like them on stage. So where do ARIA winners actually come from?

This story follows the winners of three headline awards since 1987: Album of the Year, Best Group and Best Country Album. It maps each winner's hometown, compares every state's wins with its share of the population, looks at the divide between the cities and the country, and ends with the artists who keep winning. It is written for anyone who watches the ARIAs or grew up a long drive from the nearest capital city, and you don't need a music or statistics background to follow it.

A few terms first. Hometown is the place Wikipedia lists as the artist's or band's origin; when no origin is given, we use the birthplace if it is in Australia, and for a duo or collaboration the first-named artist's hometown. Major city, inner regional, outer regional, remote and very remote are the Australian Bureau of Statistics Remoteness Areas, which group places by how far people must travel to reach services. Sydney is a major city, Kerang in Victoria is outer regional, and Milingimbi in Arnhem Land is very remote.

Part 1 · The map

Five capital cities produced 84 of the 116 wins

Start with the dots. Each circle is a hometown, sized by the number of ARIA wins its artists have taken home across our three awards.

Sydney alone holds 36 wins, more than Victoria and Western Australia combined.

Melbourne follows with 16, then Brisbane and Adelaide with 12 each and Perth with 8. Away from the coast the map goes quiet. The few inland dots are almost all country music winners, such as John Williamson from Kerang and Lee Kernaghan from Corryong.

Use the buttons under the map to see each award on its own. Album of the Year has one winner a year, so across 39 years it lands on many hometowns; pick one decade in the Years menu to see just that period. Album of the Year and Best Group barely leave the capitals.

Raw totals favour big states, so the two maps above adjust for population. New South Wales stays strong: 46 wins from 31% of Australians. South Australia looks even stronger, but 11 of its 13 wins belong to one artist, Kasey Chambers.

Queensland and Western Australia fall below their fair share, and Tasmania has a single win, Casey Barnes's Best Country Album in 2022.

Part 2 · Fair shares

New South Wales has 31% of Australians and 40% of the wins

Picture all Australians as 100 squares, and all 116 wins as another 100. If the awards were spread evenly, the two grids would match.

They don't. The blue NSW block grows from 31 squares to 40. Queensland shrinks from 20 to 15 and Victoria from 26 to 21. South Australia rises from 7 to 11.

Hover any square to light up that state in both grids.

Laying the states out roughly where they sit on the map shows when each one had its turn.

Queensland won 14 times between 1990 and 2009, and has not won once in the 2020s.

The 2020s brought firsts instead: Canberra's Genesis Owusu won Album of the Year twice, Tasmania's Casey Barnes took Best Country Album, and Baker Boy brought the top prize back to the Northern Territory for the first time since 1990.

Part 3 · City lights, country roads

Outside the cities, country music is almost the only way onto the stage

About 73% of Australians live in a major city. Among Album of the Year and Best Group winners the figure is 95%: 73 of 77 wins.

Best Country Album tells a different story: 15 of its 35 wins with a recorded hometown went to artists from regional towns.

That is 43%, well above the 27% of Australians who live outside the major cities.

Now every win becomes a dot, placed by year and by how remote the winner's hometown is.

The top row overflows. The rows beneath are sprinkled with green, the colour of country music. More than half of those green dots come from before 2000, and only one appears after 2012.

Since 2017 the only winner with a recorded hometown outside a major city is Baker Boy.

Three recent country wins, by Fanny Lumsden and Casey Barnes, have only a state recorded, so the gap may be smaller than it looks.

Each state's column is as wide as its share of all wins, and split by which award those wins came from.

New South Wales wins across all three awards. South Australia's column is almost entirely green, while Western Australia has never won Best Country Album at all.

Part 4 · Dynasties

Four artists hold almost a quarter of the trophies

Here is every winner on one strip, shaded by how many wins that artist has in total. The darkest cells are the artists who keep coming back.

Album of the Year and Best Group often go to the same act: it has happened 17 times, from Midnight Oil in 1991 to Amyl and the Sniffers in 2025.

Hover a cell to light up every win by the same artist.

Kasey Chambers (11 wins), Troy Cassar-Daley and Tame Impala (6 each) and Powderfinger (5) hold 28 of the 116 wins. Forty artists won only once.

Kasey Chambers took the lead by 2010 and nobody has caught her since.

Hover a line to follow one artist's climb.

Grouping winners by state shows two kinds of success. New South Wales has a deep bench of 27 different winning artists. South Australia has three, and one of them, Kasey Chambers, fills most of the box.

Part 5 · Who's next

The spotlight moves, but it keeps returning to Sydney

A smoothed timeline for each state shows its winning streaks as ridges. The bigger the ridge, the more wins around those years.

Queensland's ridge peaks around 2000, the Powderfinger years. Western Australia rises in the 2010s with Tame Impala. New South Wales never fades for long.

The newest ridges belong to the smallest places: Canberra, Tasmania and the Northern Territory all appear in the 2020s.

Recognition follows the capitals

Five capital cities account for 72% of wins. Sydney alone has more than Victoria and Western Australia combined.

Country music carries the regions

Regional towns win Best Country Album often. Only 4 of 77 Album of the Year and Best Group wins came from outside a major city.

The newest winners come from the margins

Canberra, Tasmania and Milingimbi all won in the 2020s. Whether that continues is worth watching at the next ceremony.

Notes on method

  • The three awards were chosen because each has run every year since 1987 under a stable name. Wikipedia lists no Best Group winner for 1988, giving 116 wins.
  • Hometowns use the Wikipedia infobox "origin"; where absent, an Australian birthplace. Kasey Chambers's origin is listed as Adelaide, although she grew up on the Nullarbor and in Southend.
  • Shanley Del (born in New Zealand, no Australian origin listed) is not mapped. Fanny Lumsden and Casey Barnes have only a state listed, so they count for their state but have no point on the map.
  • Slim Dusty's birthplace, Nulla Nulla Creek, is not in the postcode list, so the nearby town of Kempsey supplies its coordinates. Amy Shark's Gold Coast uses Surfers Paradise.
  • Collaborations such as Kasey Chambers and Shane Nicholson are credited to the first-named artist.
  • Population figures are 2026 and 2023 estimates applied to all years. They describe Australia today, not at the time of each win.
